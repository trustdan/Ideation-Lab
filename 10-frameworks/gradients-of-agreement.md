# Gradients of agreement
## When to use
A high-stakes decision where you need real commitment levels, not a forced binary yes/no —
especially when people seem hesitant to speak up or a rushed consensus risks false
compliance. Skip it for quick operational calls, or when the decider has no intention of
revising the proposal based on feedback.
## Steps
1. Post the proposal and an 8-point scale: 1 wholehearted endorsement → 4 neutral ("I'll go
along") → 5–6 formal disagreement with/without support → 8 block. 2. Read the proposal aloud,
2 min. 3. Simultaneous vote on a 3-2-1 count, not sequentially — sequential voting invites
copying senior voices, 3 min. 4. Levels 1–4 = support carries forward; levels 5–8 = invite
those voters to name the specific change that would move them to a 3+, don't debate, just
capture, 10–20 min. 5. Amend and revote if the change is feasible, 10 min.
## Output
A numerical map of real alignment and an updated proposal, ready for owners and next steps.
## Facilitation notes
Levels 5–6 are a negotiation signal, not a full block — only 7–8 actually stop the proposal.
For large groups, use a polling tool (Slido/Mentimeter) for an instant histogram instead of a
physical board. Developed by Sam Kaner (Community at Work); this note adapts the scale, not
the full method.
