# Sailboat retrospective
## When to use
Closing a sprint, project phase, or event when the group is tired of plain "what went
well/what didn't" retros, or when you need forward-looking risks alongside historical
reflection. Skip it for strict chronological incident root-cause work, or a group that finds
visual metaphors distracting (use plain Start/Stop/Continue columns instead).
## Steps
1. Draw four quadrants: Island (goal), Wind (what drove us forward), Anchor (what held us
back), Rocks (risks ahead). 2. Silent sticky-note writing, one idea per note, 10 min, across
all four. 3. Post and cluster aloud, 20 min. 4. Dot-vote the top Anchors and Rocks to
address, 3–5 dots each, 5 min. 5. Action-plan the top 2–3 voted items with a named owner, 15
min.
## Output
A prioritized list of anchors/risks with owners, ready for the project tracker → feeds
`50-actions.md`.
## Facilitation notes
Push past vague anchors ("bad communication") with a concrete-example ask: "where exactly did
that break down this week?" Don't close the retro until every action item has a name attached
— an unowned action is a wish, not a commitment. Large groups (12+): break into 4–5 person
sub-boats, bring only the top 2 items per group back to the room. Popularized via Luke
Hohmann's *Innovation Games*; no affiliation implied.
