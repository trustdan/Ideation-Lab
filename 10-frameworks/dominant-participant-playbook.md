# Dominant participant playbook
## When to use
One person is taking 40–50%+ of airtime in a group of four or more, others have stopped
trying to speak, or you see crossed arms, device-checking, or abandoned attempts to jump in.
Before intervening, check: is the input actually adding unique value, or repetitive? Is the
room disengaging, or content to listen to a subject-matter expert? Did the activity leave
room open-floor when it should have forced turn-taking?
## Steps
1. Validate then redirect by name: "Thanks, Alex — you've flagged the timeline risk. Maya,
how does that land with engineering?" 2. Change modality: shift from open discussion to
silent writing or `[[note-and-vote]]`/pair share. 3. Re-enforce a speaking rule: "let's hear
from three people who haven't spoken today." 4. If it persists, intervene directly in the
moment: "I need to pause you there so the rest of the team gets their views on the table." 5.
Still unresolved: pull them aside at the next break — name the pattern neutrally, praise the
enthusiasm, and ask for their help drawing out quieter voices.
## Output
Rebalanced airtime; no file artifact.
## Facilitation notes
Never reprimand publicly, never argue the substance of what they said, and never just wait for
it to self-correct — your silence reads to the room as their contributions not mattering.
Prevent this upstream: set a "share the mic" group agreement and default to
writing-before-talking structures instead of open-floor discussion.
