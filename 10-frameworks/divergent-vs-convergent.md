# Divergent vs. convergent thinking
## When to use
Orienting a group (or yourself) on which cognitive mode a block calls for — most workshop
failures come from mixing the two, e.g. evaluating ideas while still trying to generate them.
## Steps
Divergent = generate choices, expand possibilities, "yes, and…" (`[[crazy-8s]]`,
`[[how-might-we]]`). Convergent = evaluate choices, build alignment, "which one?"
(`[[dot-voting]]`, `[[note-and-vote]]`). Between them sits Sam Kaner's **Groan Zone**: the
initial easy ideas are exhausted, perspectives diverge, and clarity hasn't arrived yet. Hold
space for it — don't rush the group to closure just to escape the discomfort.
## Output
A shared vocabulary for naming which mode a block is in, and permission to stay in the Groan
Zone instead of forcing premature convergence.
## Facilitation notes
Divergent failure state: overwhelm, no direction, endless talking with no resolution.
Convergent failure state: premature consensus, uninspired picks, groupthink. If a block feels
stuck, name which mode it's supposed to be in out loud — often that alone is the fix.
