# Premortem
## When to use
A favorite has emerged and momentum is drowning doubt — right before commitment. Skip it if
the team is already demoralized, or the plan isn't defined enough yet to have a specific
failure mode.
## Steps
1. "It's 12 months later; this failed completely." 2. Everyone writes causes of death
silently, 5 min — one per sticky note; push past superficial complaints ("we ran 2 days late"
isn't a disaster — dig for what actually got canceled). 3. Round-robin, one cause per turn
until the board's full, cluster, rank by probability × severity. 4. Top 3 get a cheap kill
test or mitigation with an owner.
## Output
Ranked failure causes + kill tests → feed the provocateur's challenge note and
`50-actions.md`.
## Facilitation notes
Past tense is mandatory ("it failed", not "it might"). The quietest skeptic goes first. Frame
it explicitly: "what could go wrong" makes people defend the plan; assuming it already failed
gives permission to say the quiet part out loud. Concept originates with Gary Klein (2007); no
affiliation implied.
