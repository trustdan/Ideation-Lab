# Personal-conflict playbook
## When to use
Critique of ideas has turned into attacks on motives, competence, or character — raised
voices, sarcastic laughter, or the rest of the room going quiet and withdrawing. First ask: is
this task conflict (healthy) or relationship conflict (destructive), and is anyone's
psychological or physical safety actually compromised?
## Steps
1. Pause the room, firmly and neutrally. 2. Reframe from people to problem: capture the
complaint as a system issue on the board, not a statement about the person. 3. Point back to
the group's working agreements ("attack the problem, not the person"). Script: "Let's pause —
we're focusing on individuals, but our job is the underlying process. What's the systemic
bottleneck affecting both teams right now?"
## Output
De-escalated room, returned to task focus; no file artifact.
## Facilitation notes
Escalation ladder if the pause doesn't hold: a 10-minute break to cool off → a private
conversation with the individuals during the break → pausing the session and looping in the
executive sponsor or HR if hostility persists. Never take sides, never publicly shame the
offending participant, never assume it'll self-correct. Prevent upstream with explicit working
agreements and silent-writing structures that separate ideas from the people holding them.
Framing draws on Patterson et al.'s *Crucial Conversations*.
