# Community engagement / service-design clinic

**Who it's for:** A public agency or nonprofit program with low uptake or low trust from the
community it's meant to serve — outreach isn't converting, or the people eligible for a service
aren't using it. Participants span program staff, frontline outreach workers, and — wherever
possible — members of the community the program serves.
**Outcome it produces:** A bet on the single biggest access or trust gap, shaped by input from
people actually affected, plus a near-term pilot and owner. Not an awareness campaign; a
concrete change to how the program engages.
**Envelope:** half-day to 1 day (~4–6 working hours, scale to headcount) · 8–15 participants ·
in-person strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator
- **Do not run this session without at least one voice from the community the program serves in
  the room** — either directly, or via pre-session interviews the facilitator brings in as
  material if attendance genuinely isn't possible. Designing engagement fixes with only program
  staff in the room reproduces the exact gap the session exists to close.
- **Known failure mode #1:** low uptake gets attributed to the community ("they don't know
  about it," "they don't prioritize it") rather than to structural barriers — trust, past harm,
  stigma, language, transportation, hours that don't match people's actual schedules. Push the
  HMW round past awareness framings explicitly.
- **Known failure mode #2:** program staff over-index on the loudest community feedback (a
  single vocal complaint or a single enthusiastic endorsement) instead of the pattern across
  many quieter voices. If community members are in the room, protect quieter voices from being
  drowned out the same way [[note-and-vote]] protects frontline staff from a room's senior
  voices — use it for convergence if the room has any status imbalance, which it likely does.
- Distrust of the institution itself — not just the specific program — may be the real barrier.
  Naming that directly, without defensiveness, tends to unlock more honest discussion than
  treating it as an unfortunate perception problem to be corrected with messaging.
- If a community member's account of harm or mistreatment surfaces, do not move past it to stay
  on schedule — it is very likely exactly the kind of information this session exists to
  surface. `attribution: anonymous` (CLAUDE.md guardrail 5) is a strong default here, but
  confirm with any community participants what level of attribution they're actually
  comfortable with — don't assume anonymity is what they want either.
