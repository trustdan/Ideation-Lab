# Enterprise GTM strategy clinic

**Who it's for:** A B2B enterprise sales or commercial leadership team facing a shift that
makes the current go-to-market motion unreliable — a new competitor with a cheaper wedge, a
pricing model under pressure, a flagship segment that's stalled, or a channel/partner
relationship that's no longer differentiating.
**Outcome it produces:** A prioritized GTM bet — the segment or motion it's aimed at, the
positioning claim behind it, and a near-term pipeline/enablement tactics list with named owners
and dates. Not a rebrand; a bet the sales org actually changes behavior for.
**Envelope:** 1 day (~6 working hours) · 8–15 participants · in-person strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator
- Participants live in quota and pipeline-coverage thinking and will reflexively route open
  discussion through "will this help me hit number this quarter." Protect the early divergent
  blocks explicitly — tell the room the tactics conversation comes later, after the bet is
  chosen, not before.
- **Known failure mode #1:** the room converges on "sell harder / better enablement / more
  discounting" as a safe answer that avoids picking an actual target segment or repositioning.
  Provoke this early, not just at the end — it is the GTM-clinic analogue of
  [[defense-contractor-strategy]]'s "chase more IDIQs."
- **Known failure mode #2:** a competitor's pricing move gets treated as the whole problem,
  crowding out any discussion of what the competitor is actually offering that customers value.
  Push the room past price in the HMW round.
- Sales leadership and individual reps often disagree sharply about which segment is winnable —
  reps are closer to the actual objections; leadership is closer to the strategic rationale.
  Make sure both get real airtime before the dot vote, not just leadership's framing.
- Candor about which reps/segments are underperforming depends on psychological safety when
  quota-holders are in the room together. This is a reasonable default case for
  `attribution: anonymous` (CLAUDE.md guardrail 5).
