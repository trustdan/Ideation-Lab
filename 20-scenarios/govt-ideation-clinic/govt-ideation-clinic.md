# Government ideation clinic

**Who it's for:** A federal, state, or local government agency (non-defense) tackling a policy
or service-delivery challenge — a permitting backlog, a digital service that isn't landing, an
interagency data-sharing gap — that wants a structured ideation session rather than another
working group. The generic entry point for "any government agency wants to run this," the way
[[example-product-offsite]] is the generic entry point for product-tech.
**Outcome it produces:** 2–3 committed initiative bets with owners and kill tests attached, each
checked against what the agency can actually pilot without a new procurement action. Not a
policy white paper; initiatives someone will actually start Monday.
**Envelope:** half-day (~4 working hours) · 6–12 participants · in-person or remote.

## Notes for the facilitator
- **Known failure mode #1:** divergence gets suppressed early by imagined constraints —
  "legal won't let us," "procurement takes two years" — that no one in the room has actually
  confirmed. Pre-clear real boundaries with a legal or procurement point of contact before the
  session, the same discipline [[defense-contractor-strategy]] applies to classification
  concerns, so the room can be told which constraints are real and which aren't.
- **Known failure mode #2:** public accountability risk ("what if this fails and it's in the
  news" / "what if this gets FOIA'd") silently narrows every idea to the safest possible option
  before anyone says the fear out loud. Name it directly at the open rather than letting it
  operate as an unstated filter on every HMW stem.
- Career staff and political appointees often have very different time horizons in the room. An
  idea that excites a short-tenure appointee needs a career-staff sponsor to survive past a
  leadership change — ask explicitly, per committed bet, who owns it after the current
  administration or leadership term ends.
- Kill tests matter more here than in most scenarios: a government initiative that quietly
  limps along unevaluated for years is a more common failure mode than one that's visibly
  cancelled. Insist on a specific, dated check-in for every committed bet.
