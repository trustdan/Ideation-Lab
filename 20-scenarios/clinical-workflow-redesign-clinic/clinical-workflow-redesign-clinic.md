# Clinical workflow redesign clinic

**Who it's for:** A hospital department, nursing unit, or clinic (ED, inpatient floor, primary
care practice) whose day-to-day workflow has accumulated workarounds — competing priorities
from clinicians, administration, and compliance have each left their mark, and no one owns the
whole thing anymore.
**Outcome it produces:** One prioritized workflow change, a named pilot unit or shift to try it
on, and an owner. Not a process-mapping exercise; a change the unit is willing to actually try.
**Envelope:** half-day (~4 working hours) · 8–14 participants spanning roles (physicians,
nurses, techs, admin, compliance) · in-person strongly preferred — this population is shift-
based and rarely all in one room, so make the room count.

## Notes for the facilitator
- Role hierarchy is load-bearing here in a way it isn't in most scenarios: physicians speaking
  first will anchor nurses and techs even when the tech is the one who actually does the
  workaround daily. Converge with [[note-and-vote]] instead of open discussion or a raw dot
  vote — the silent-write step exists specifically so the person closest to the workaround
  states a position before the room's senior voice does.
- **Known failure mode #1:** the room converges on "more training" or "better compliance
  messaging" as a safe answer that avoids redesigning the actual workflow. This is this
  scenario's version of [[defense-contractor-strategy]]'s "chase more IDIQs" — provoke it in
  the HMW round, not just at the end.
- **Known failure mode #2:** "patient safety" gets invoked to shut down speculative discussion
  before it's actually tested against a real safety constraint. Pre-clear the room's genuine
  hard constraints (what truly cannot change without a formal safety review) in the session's
  Constraints section — don't let a soft objection get litigated live as if it were a hard one.
- Staff pulled from different shifts may never have met. If a shift is unrepresented in the
  room, say so explicitly and treat any bet touching that shift as provisional pending their
  input, not as decided on their behalf.
- Frontline staff critiquing a workflow a physician designed or a compliance rule they didn't
  write depends on psychological safety. This is a strong default case for
  `attribution: anonymous` (CLAUDE.md guardrail 5).
