# Design sprint 2.0

**Who it's for:** a product team (5–7, including one decider who can actually rule) with a
specific, testable question about a new or existing product direction — not a vague "let's
innovate" ask. Best when real uncertainty exists that a prototype and five user interviews can
resolve, not something already knowable from existing data.
**Outcome it produces:** validated / refuted / inconclusive verdicts against the sprint's own
questions, backed by five real user interviews against a prototype, plus one recommended next
step. No code shipped — a façade, not a build.
**Envelope:** 4 consecutive days (~24 working hours; see [[design-sprint-2-agenda]] for the
~7h/6h/6h/5h split) · 5–7 participants + 1 decider + 5 external test interviewees on Day 4 ·
in-person strongly preferred — the sketching and prototyping days lose a lot remote.

## Notes for the facilitator

- The compressed 4-day version merges Monday and Tuesday (see [[design-sprint-2-agenda]]) —
  don't let Day 1 balloon back into two days out of caution. If the room needs the classic
  orientation first, walk them through [[design-sprint-day-by-day]] before adapting to this
  pack's compression.
- **Known failure mode #1:** the decider is "available all week" but not actually in the room
  for the Day 2 decide gate. Confirm real calendar blocking before Day 1, not the morning of —
  this is exactly what the coach's prep-mode checklist's Decider section exists to catch.
- **Known failure mode #2:** recruiting slips and Day 4 tests whoever's available (a coworker,
  a friend) instead of the real target profile. Run the coach's prep-mode screener; don't
  substitute convenience for the profile.
- If a day runs over, consult [[time-recovery-playbook]] before improvising a cut — protect the
  Day 2 decide gate and all of Day 4 above everything else.
- This scenario supports the Design Sprint methodology (Knapp, *Sprint*, 2016) as a
  facilitation frame; no affiliation with GV or AJ&Smart is implied.

## Long-term goal
<!-- Fill in before Day 1: the multi-month outcome this sprint's product direction is meant to
move — not this week's output, the destination that makes this week worth running. -->
In 12 months, self-serve signups reach a working "aha" moment inside their first session without
touching a salesperson or a help article, measured by day-1 activation rate and 30-day retention
of self-serve accounts.

## Sprint questions
<!-- Fill in before Day 1: the specific, falsifiable questions Friday's prototype test must
answer. Each becomes a `test-scenario` note once the session exists; the synthesizer answers
each one explicitly and cites evidence for every non-`pending` verdict. -->
- Will a new self-serve user understand the core value prop within 10 seconds of landing on the
  onboarding flow, without any tooltip or help text?
- Will a new user complete the key first task (creating their first project) without hitting the
  integration-setup step that today causes the most drop-off?
- Will a new user choose the guided setup path over skipping straight to a blank workspace when
  both are shown up front?
