# Interview screener — Design sprint 2.0

Use this document during recruiting to qualify Day 4 test participants. Each question maps to
one sprint question and is written to disqualify the wrong participant fast. Read alongside
`prep-checklist.md`'s Screener section, which explains the disqualify-on logic in full.

---

## Inclusion criteria
A participant must meet **all** of the following to be scheduled:

1. Has personally signed up for at least one SaaS product on a self-serve basis (no sales
   assist, no IT-provisioned account) within the last 90 days.
2. Can recall and describe a specific recent onboarding experience in detail — not just "yeah
   I use a lot of apps."
3. Has reached the point of attempting a first meaningful task in that product (not just
   created an account and bounced before seeing anything).
4. Is available for a 60-minute one-on-one session on the Day 4 date, on-site or via
   screenshare as the sprint setup requires.

## Exclusion criteria
Disqualify if **any** of the following are true:

- Their SaaS signups are always handled by IT, an admin, or a sales-assisted trial — they
  don't personally navigate onboarding.
- They work for the product company being tested, or a direct competitor in a role that would
  make them unrepresentative (sales engineer, implementation consultant, etc.).
- They have never encountered an integration or account-connection step during onboarding
  (makes sprint question 2 untestable with them).
- They cannot recall any specific past instance — only hypothetical or general answers.
  Stated intent is not a substitute for past behavior.

---

## Screening questions

Ask in this order. Each question maps to one sprint question. Stop and disqualify as soon as
a disqualify trigger is hit — don't complete the full screener with an unqualified candidate.

### Q1 — Maps to sprint question 1
*(Will a new self-serve user understand the core value prop within 10 seconds of landing on
the onboarding flow, without any tooltip or help text?)*

> "In the last 90 days, have you personally signed up for a new SaaS product on your own —
> without a demo, a trial setup call, or a sales rep involved? Walk me through the last time
> it happened: what made you sign up, and what did you do in the first few minutes after
> landing on the product for the first time?"

**Disqualify on:** No recent self-serve signup / can't recall a specific instance / signup was
IT-provisioned or sales-assisted / they skipped past the landing screen without reading it
because IT pre-configured everything.

### Q2 — Maps to sprint question 2
*(Will a new user complete the key first task — creating their first project — without hitting
the integration-setup step that today causes the most drop-off?)*

> "Think about the last time you started using a new tool and were asked to connect it to
> something else you already use — like linking an account, setting up an integration, or
> importing data from another system — before you could actually do anything. Did you complete
> that step, skip it, or abandon the product? Walk me through what happened."

**Disqualify on:** Has never encountered an integration/setup gate during onboarding / always
has IT or an admin handle integrations (they're not the person making the decision in the
moment the gate appears).

### Q3 — Maps to sprint question 3
*(Will a new user choose the guided setup path over skipping straight to a blank workspace
when both are shown up front?)*

> "When you sign up for a new tool that offers a walkthrough or guided setup — and also lets
> you skip straight to the product — do you take the walkthrough or skip it? Describe the
> last time you made that choice: what did you pick, and what was going through your head?"

**Disqualify on:** Always delegates setup to someone else and has no personal memory of
making this choice / answer is purely hypothetical with no specific instance / they are a
self-described "always skip, never read tutorials" power user AND the sprint is explicitly
trying to reach mainstream/new users (include this profile only if the team has decided
that segment is in scope).

---

## After qualifying a participant
- Confirm Day 4 date, time, and location (or screenshare link).
- Send a brief, neutral confirmation: *"We'll be showing you an early design and asking for
  your honest reactions. There are no right or wrong answers — we're testing the design, not
  you."* Do not mention the product name or what the prototype shows; you want their first
  reaction in Act 3 of the interview to be unprimed.
- Log their name, the specific past-behavior answer from Q1 that qualified them, and any
  relevant context from Q2/Q3 — this feeds the note-taker's baseline in Act 2 of the
  interview script.
