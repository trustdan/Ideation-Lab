## Recruit
- **Core team, Days 1–3:** 5–7 people plus the decider (the decider can be one of the 5–7 or a
  separate stakeholder who joins for the Day 2 gate and Day 4 synthesis — see Decider below).
- **External test interviewees, Day 4:** 5, matching the "[target user] doing [job]" language
  in the hub's Long-term-goal once it's filled in — not a coworker or friend of convenience.
  That substitution is this pack's Known failure mode #2. Five is the Design Sprint / usability
  convention: enough to surface the dominant patterns without drowning Day 4's live synthesis
  in more transcript than can be reviewed same-day.
- **Best-fit profile:** people who currently live with the friction the long-term goal names —
  ideally people who've already built a workaround for it (this ties directly to the
  prompt-pack's warm-up question, which asks interviewees to describe exactly that workaround).
  Someone who's never encountered the problem can't validate or refute a sprint question about
  it.
- **Lead time:** external recruiting for Day 4 typically needs 1–2 weeks. Start it in parallel
  with Day 1 planning, not after Day 2 once the direction is picked — the screener questions
  below can be drafted before the direction is known, since they're keyed to the sprint
  questions, not the winning storyboard.
- **Backups:** line up 1–2 alternates for Day 4 no-shows. Five clean interviews beats five
  merely-scheduled ones.

## Decider
- Name the specific person, by role, who can make the Day 2 supervote call and rule on the
  Day 4 synthesis. "Available all week" is not confirmation — get explicit calendar blocking
  for those two specific moments, in writing, before Day 1. This is the hub's Known failure
  mode #1 and the exact gap this section exists to catch.
- If the real decider genuinely cannot attend one of those moments, name their delegate in
  advance, with the same authority, rather than discovering the gap live.
- The decider needs to be *in the room* for the supervote, not briefed afterward — the
  method's whole premise (Knapp, *Sprint*) is a single accountable person cutting design-by-
  committee debate short, live.

## Room & supplies
- One room the team holds for all 4 consecutive days without being displaced — storyboards,
  the target map, and dot-vote sheets need to stay on the walls overnight between days.
- Wall/whiteboard space for: expert-interview notes and the target map (Day 1); a storyboard
  gallery large enough for a silent heat-map critique and dot voting (Day 2).
- Supplies: sticky notes, dot stickers (or markers as a substitute) for voting, sharpies,
  large paper or whiteboard surface for storyboards, a visible timer.
- Day 3 façade build: whatever tool suits the prototype — a no-code tool (Figma, Keynote,
  a clickable mockup) is normal and expected; this is a façade, not a build (per the hub).
- Day 4: a separate, quieter space for the one-on-one interviews, plus a way for the rest of
  the team to watch live (a side-room video feed, a one-way-glass-style setup, or just
  everyone quietly in the same room if the interviewee is comfortable with it) — the framework
  note is explicit that team reactions during the interview are as valuable as the transcript.

## Screener
- Write one screening question per sprint question, once the hub's Sprint questions section
  is filled in for the real engagement — each question's job is to disqualify the wrong
  participant fast, not to confirm demographics.
- Prefer past-behavior questions over stated-intent ones: "tell me about the last time you did
  X" screens harder than "would you do X?" — this mirrors the prompt-pack's own warm-up
  question, which asks for a specific past instance, not a hypothetical.
- Include at least one question aimed at the false-positive case: someone who already works
  around the friction, but through a channel that makes them unrepresentative of the target
  job (a power user, an internal employee, a consultant who's seen every competitor) — unless
  that specific profile *is* the target, screen them out.
- Template shape per sprint question: "In the last [timeframe], have you personally
  [behavior implied by the sprint question]? Walk me through the last time it happened." →
  disqualify on "no," or on an answer that reveals a different job-to-be-done than the sprint
  question assumes.
