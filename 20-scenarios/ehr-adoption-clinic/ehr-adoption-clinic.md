# EHR / health-tech adoption clinic

**Who it's for:** A clinical informatics or IT team that has rolled out a new EHR module or
clinical decision-support tool that clinicians are working around — shadow paper charts,
alert-dismissal on autopilot, or quiet non-use. The healthcare-specific sibling of
[[platform-adoption-clinic]]: same shape, different stakes (liability and patient time, not
just workflow friction).
**Outcome it produces:** One prioritized adoption bet — the specific friction it removes, the
clinician group or service line it targets first, and a rollout owner with a next-90-days plan.
**Envelope:** half-day (~4 working hours) · 6–12 participants (informatics/IT, clinician
champions, and frontline clinicians who actually use the tool) · in-person or remote.

## Notes for the facilitator
- Distinguish "clinicians are being stubborn" from "the tool asks too much of a 12-minute
  visit" before the room does anything else. Alert fatigue and click burden are the two most
  common root causes hiding under a generic "adoption" complaint — the HMW round should surface
  which one this is before proposing fixes.
- **Known failure mode #1:** the informatics team treats resistance as a training or
  communications gap ("they just need to learn the new workflow") rather than a design or time-
  cost problem — the same failure mode [[platform-adoption-clinic]] names, sharpened here by
  the fact that clinician time is a harder constraint than almost any other role's.
- **Known failure mode #2:** a legitimate liability or documentation-defensibility concern
  ("I could get sued if I don't chart it this way") gets raised, correctly, and then gets used
  to shut down all speculative discussion of the tool, including parts unrelated to that
  concern. Pre-clear which specific requirements are genuinely non-negotiable (usually a small,
  named list) so the rest of the room can still explore freely — the same discipline
  [[defense-contractor-strategy]] applies to classification concerns.
- Any adoption bet has to be evaluated against added clicks or minutes per encounter, given
  already-thin visit-time margins — make this an explicit line in the bet-brief, not an
  afterthought raised in the premortem.
- Clinician champions in the room may not represent the median clinician's tolerance for change
  — actively invite the room's most skeptical clinician to speak before converging.
