# Patient access & care-navigation clinic

**Who it's for:** A health system or multi-site practice losing patients between referral and
appointment — scheduling bottlenecks, no-shows, and hand-offs across departments that lose
patients rather than routing them. Participants span scheduling/front-desk staff, care
coordinators, referring and receiving clinicians, and ops leadership.
**Outcome it produces:** A prioritized bet on the single biggest drop-off point — the segment
or referral pathway it targets, and a near-term tactics list with named owners and dates. Not a
full access-strategy overhaul; the one gap worth fixing first.
**Envelope:** 1 day (~6 working hours) · 8–15 participants · in-person strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator
- **Known failure mode #1:** the room defaults to framing drop-off as a patient behavior
  problem ("no-shows are just no-shows") instead of a system problem. Push past patient-blaming
  framings explicitly in the HMW round — ask what the system asked of the patient that it
  didn't need to.
- **Known failure mode #2:** departments blame each other's software or process for the
  drop-off without anyone owning the handoff itself. If two departments each believe the other
  is the bottleneck, that's a signal the actual gap is the handoff, not either department.
- Data is often thinner than participants assume — many systems can't cleanly report where in
  the referral-to-appointment pipeline patients actually drop off. If clean data doesn't exist,
  say so at the open and use a rough process map (walk the pathway step by step, ask "where do
  we lose people here") instead of asking the room to defer to numbers that don't exist yet.
- Access gaps frequently correlate with transportation, language, insurance status, and other
  social determinants of health. Name this as a lens the room should apply to whichever gap it
  picks, not a separate topic to bolt on if time allows.
- Front-desk and scheduling staff are closest to the actual friction but often have the least
  seniority in the room — protect their airtime the same way [[note-and-vote]] would in a
  hierarchy-heavy clinical room; use it if the room skews senior.
