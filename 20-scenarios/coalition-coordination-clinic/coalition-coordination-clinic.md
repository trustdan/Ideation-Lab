# Cross-agency / coalition coordination clinic

**Who it's for:** Leads from multiple agencies or nonprofits with overlapping mandates —
housing, homelessness services, workforce development, and similar — who duplicate effort in
some areas and leave gaps in others because no one has explicitly divided the work. The
public-sector sibling of [[reorg-integration-clinic]]: same shape, different root cause (mission
overlap and funding competition between separate organizations, not a single org's merger).
**Outcome it produces:** A named coordination bet per overlapping mandate area — which org
leads, who defers and how, and an explicit list of what is deliberately *not* being decided
today. Not a memorandum of understanding; the first real commitments toward one.
**Envelope:** 1 day (~6 working hours) · 8–16 participants across organizations · in-person
strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator
- Name the financial incentive misalignment directly and early: separate organizations often
  compete for the same grant pool, which makes "coordinate instead of duplicate" go against
  each org's own funding interest. Goodwill alone does not fix this — the session needs to find
  bets that work even given that incentive, not pretend it away.
- **Known failure mode #1:** organizations defend their current scope as mission-critical
  ("only we can do this part right") the same way [[reorg-integration-clinic]] sees "our way
  was better" advocacy — evaluate scope claims on service-gap evidence, not on which org states
  them more confidently.
- **Known failure mode #2:** the org with more funding, more staff, or more political standing
  in the room has structural power regardless of whether its mandate claim is actually
  stronger. Converge with [[note-and-vote]] and run the decide gate with
  [[gradients-of-agreement]] rather than an open vote, so a smaller org's real disagreement (a 5
  or 6, not a full block) gets captured rather than steamrolled.
- Ground the discussion in specific people, not abstractions: ask for a real (anonymized) case
  where someone needed services from two or more orgs in the room and fell through the gap
  between them. That case is usually the sharpest test of any proposed coordination bet.
- Turf and funding-formula questions can consume the entire day if left open-ended. Agree the
  specific mandate areas in scope with leadership before the session, and explicitly park
  anything else that surfaces — see references.md.
