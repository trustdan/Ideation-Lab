# Grant / funding strategy clinic

**Who it's for:** A nonprofit or public agency facing a funding cliff — an expiring flagship
grant, donor fatigue, a budget cut, or over-reliance on a single funding source. Participants
span executive leadership, program staff, and development/board members.
**Outcome it produces:** A funding strategy bet — the program or funding stream it's aimed at,
the case for it, and a near-term ask list with named owners and dates. Not a capital-campaign
deck; a decision leadership actually made.
**Envelope:** 1 day (~6 working hours) · 8–15 participants · in-person strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator
- **Known failure mode #1:** the room converges on "write more grant applications" as a safe,
  false-consensus answer that avoids an actual strategic bet — this scenario's version of
  [[defense-contractor-strategy]]'s "chase more IDIQs." Provoke it early.
- **Known failure mode #2:** program staff and development staff talk past each other because
  "what we're good at" (mission-fit) and "what funders will pay for" (funder-fit) get treated
  as the same question. Force the HMW round to name which one a given idea is actually
  answering.
- Board members sometimes push for a single mega-gift or capital campaign as a silver bullet
  without addressing the structural over-reliance that created the cliff in the first place.
  Name this pattern if it surfaces — it's a bet on luck, not a strategy.
- If the funding cliff implies program cuts or layoffs, that's a real possibility staff in the
  room may already sense. Be explicit at the open about what this session will and won't
  decide, and default to `attribution: anonymous` (CLAUDE.md guardrail 5) — candor about which
  programs are vulnerable depends on it.
- Watch for mission drift: a funding source that pays well but pulls the organization away from
  its actual mission is a real cost, not a free win — the premortem block should test for it
  explicitly.
