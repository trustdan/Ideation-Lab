# Defense contractor strategy clinic

**Who it's for:** Small to mid-size defense and government-services contractors (roughly
8–200 employees) facing a strategic inflection point — recompete pressure on a flagship
contract, over-reliance on one or two primes, a shift in agency mission priorities, or a
first-time pursuit of a new customer set or contract vehicle.
**Outcome it produces:** A prioritized strategic bet (or two) — a positioning statement, the
target mission/customer set it's aimed at, and a near-term capture/BD tactics list with named
owners and dates. Not a vision deck; a decision the leadership team actually made.
**Envelope:** 1 day (~6 working hours) · 8–15 participants · in-person strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator

- Participants live in capture-plan rigor (color-team reviews, PWin scoring, shipley-style
  gates) and will reflexively try to route open discussion through that lens. Protect the
  early divergent blocks explicitly — tell the room compliance-matrix thinking comes later.
- **Known failure mode #1:** the room converges on "chase more IDIQs / get on more vehicles"
  as a safe, false-consensus answer that avoids an actual bet. Provoke this early, not just
  at the end.
- **Known failure mode #2:** classification or competitive-sensitivity concerns get invoked
  to shut down speculative discussion ("can't talk about that here"). Pre-clear the room's
  actual discussion boundary in the session's Constraints — don't let it get litigated live.
- Candor here depends on psychological safety — senior leaders are in the room alongside BD
  staff who report to them. This scenario is a strong default case for `attribution:
  anonymous` (CLAUDE.md guardrail 5); don't casually opt into `open`.
