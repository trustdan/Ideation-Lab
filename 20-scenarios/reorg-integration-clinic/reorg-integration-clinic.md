# Reorg / M&A integration clinic

**Who it's for:** Leads from two previously separate teams or organizations (post-acquisition,
post-merger, or a deliberate internal reorg) who now own overlapping processes, tools, or
mandates and have to decide what survives, what merges, and what gets retired.
**Outcome it produces:** A named integration bet per overlapping area — which team's process
wins, what changes for the other, and who owns the transition — plus an explicit list of what
is deliberately *not* being decided today. Not a full org chart; the first real commitments.
**Envelope:** 1 day (~6 working hours) · 8–16 participants (both sides represented roughly
evenly) · in-person strongly preferred.

## Notes for the facilitator
- This is the highest psychological-safety-risk scenario in the catalog. Participants may
  reasonably read this session as adjacent to headcount decisions even when it isn't. Set
  `attribution: anonymous` (CLAUDE.md guardrail 5) as the default and say out loud, at the
  open, what this session will and will not decide (role/headcount calls are explicitly out of
  scope unless leadership has said otherwise in writing).
- **Known failure mode #1:** the room defaults to "our way was better" advocacy from both sides
  instead of evaluating the overlap on its merits. [[note-and-vote]]'s silent-write step exists
  specifically to surface a position before anyone has had the chance to anchor the room by
  speaking first.
- **Known failure mode #2:** a quiet, unresolved disagreement gets papered over as false
  consensus to avoid conflict in a tense room — the opposite failure mode from #1, and the
  reason this pack uses [[gradients-of-agreement]] instead of a simple show of hands at the
  decide gate. A 5 or 6 is a real signal, not agreement.
- Whoever is "the acquirer" or "the surviving org" in the room dynamic will have structural
  power even if the facilitator treats both sides as equals on paper. Actively invite
  disagreement from the less-powerful side before closing any given item.
- If a specific overlap turns out to be a genuine headcount or reporting-line question in
  disguise, name that explicitly and park it — don't let the group discover mid-session that
  they've been deciding something they don't have the standing to decide.
