# 2026-07-30 Strategy Clinic — Summary

**Session:** Strategy Clinic, 2026-07-30
**Scenario:** Defense contractor strategy clinic
**Goal:** Land on a prioritized strategic bet — positioning, target mission/customer set, and
near-term capture tactics — for the next 18 months, given recompete pressure on a flagship
contract and heavy reliance on a single prime.
**Success looks like:** Leadership leaves with 1–2 named strategic bets, not just more
discussion. Each bet has an owner and a first concrete action with a date.

---

## Read me first

The pool says the room already did most of the hard work itself: eighteen ideas resolve into
three concrete, dot-voted bet-briefs (adjacent-mission cyber range, second-prime courting,
in-house cleared-talent bench) plus a standing warning — voiced three separate times today in
three different debriefs — that this company diagnoses its own weaknesses correctly and then
doesn't act on the fix. The central tension is that the most exciting bet (adjacent-mission
cyber range, the dot-vote landslide) is also the one with the least existing traction (a
genuine cold start), while the safer, cheaper bet (courting a second prime) got real but lesser
conviction and carries its own credibility risk. What had to be decided was not which idea is
cleverest — it's which 1–2 of these three already-shaped bets get an owner, a first action, and
a date, and whether this time the follow-through problem gets solved alongside them.

## Themes

1. **Reduce single-prime exposure through a second channel** — strongest variant: deliberately
   court a named second prime for 12 months, positioned as "the reliable sub who never
   no-bids you" (9 dots, advanced as Bet B, senior BD time only, no new headcount).
2. **Expand into an adjacent mission or offering** — strongest variant: pursue cyber range
   support in an adjacent agency as an accreditation subcontractor to the incumbent range
   integrator (11 dots, the clear winner of the entire shortlist, advanced as Bet A).
3. **Build the cleared-talent bench internally** — strongest variant: sponsor clearances for
   junior hires in-house with a contractual commitment period (8 dots, advanced as Bet C, but
   reclassified by the room as an ops initiative rather than a competing strategic bet).
4. **Close the loop on diagnosed weaknesses** — three separate past contract losses whose
   debriefs each named the correct fix, and none of which the org ever acted on. Not
   dot-voted; used as the day's framing device instead, and the risk it names is inherited by
   whatever gets committed from the other three themes.

## Gaps

## Recommendation & decision framing

**What was decided:** which 1–2 of the three shaped bet-briefs — the cyber-range
adjacent-mission bet (A) and the second-prime courting bet (B) — become the named strategic
bet(s) for the next 18 months, with the cleared-talent bench (C) funded in parallel as an ops
initiative regardless, and with the follow-through problem addressed explicitly rather than
left as background risk.

**The real options considered:**

1. Commit to Bet A alone (adjacent-mission cyber range) — highest upside and excitement, but a
   genuine cold start.
2. Commit to Bet B alone (second-prime courting) — lower resourcing ask, lower excitement,
   real relationship-optics risk.
3. Run both A and B in parallel through cheap kill tests before committing further capacity to
   either.
4. Reject both new-channel bets and defend the flagship recompete directly instead.

**What actually happened:** the synthesis initially recommended option 3 — running both bets'
kill tests in parallel. A red-team pass against that recommendation found a cheaper,
higher-value question nobody had asked yet: the flagship recompete's own timeline had never
been pulled, and neither had several other disqualifying facts (the target agency's real
budget window, whether the incumbent integrator has an actual accreditation gap, this
company's real bid/no-bid history with its current prime). The plan below reflects that
correction — five inexpensive pre-checks now precede either bet's original kill test.

## Decisions & actions

**Decision 1 — pre-checks before either kill test, not parallel kill tests as first drafted.**
Run the cheapest test that could kill a bet outright before spending BD capacity on the more
expensive test already designed for it.

**Decision 2 — the cleared-talent bench proceeds regardless, as an ops initiative, not a
competing strategic bet.**

**Decision 3 — follow-through gets an independent check, not just an owner and a date.** A
recurring checkpoint owned by someone with no stake in either bet, not a self-policed
reminder.

**Parked, not dropped:** selling the sustainment data layer instead of labor — real verbal
support at the decide gate, no dot-vote backing; revisit next quarterly session.

## Appendix — ideas by theme (titles only)

**Reduce single-prime exposure through a second channel**
- Deliberately court a second prime for 12 months before we need them
- Sub to a different prime in the same mission area to spread single-prime risk
- Market ourselves explicitly as a surge sub for primes with a thin bench
- Go direct on small prime awards under $10M, where the past-performance bar is lower
- Small-business set-aside status is a wedge to press, not just a ceiling to escape

**Expand into an adjacent mission or offering**
- Pursue cyber range support in an adjacent agency as our first adjacent-mission bet
- Sell the sustainment data layer, not the labor layer
- Reposition the whole company as a data company, not a labor company
- Extend into training and simulation work adjacent to current O&M contracts
- A dot-vote winner can reflect excitement rather than buildability
- The safer bet is doubling down on the existing prime relationship, not chasing an unproven
  adjacent agency

**Build the cleared-talent bench internally**
- Grow our own cleared bench by sponsoring clearances in-house
- Build a part-time, fractional bench of cleared consultants
- Tie retention bonuses to contract wins, not just tenure
- Flexible or remote work, not comp, is the top lever for retaining cleared talent
- Structural/ops bets need different evaluation criteria than BD capture bets

**Close the loop on diagnosed weaknesses**
- Loss debriefs keep naming the real fix, and the org keeps not doing it
- Proposals lose when they're written to the customer's old CONOPS instead of their current one
