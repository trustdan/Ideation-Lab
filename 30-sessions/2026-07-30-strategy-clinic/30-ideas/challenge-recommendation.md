# Challenge — the recommendation (run both kill tests in parallel)

## Assumption inventory

1. Both kill tests are roughly equivalent in cost and in what a "pass" means, so running
   them in parallel is a fair, low-risk way to defer the real choice.
2. Senior BD capacity is fungible enough to run two external probes at once without
   opportunity cost to anything else BD should be doing right now — including the flagship
   recompete.
3. Bet A's target program office starting "budget planning next fiscal year" actually lands
   inside the 18-month strategic window — this is listed as something to *confirm*, not
   something confirmed, yet the recommendation proceeds as if it's a settled input.
4. The flagship recompete's own timeline is far enough out (or well enough resourced
   already) that it can absorb attention being spent on two new-channel probes this quarter.
5. "Assign an owner and a 30-day check-in date" is sufficient to break the diagnose-and-
   don't-act pattern Theme 4 names — i.e., that the mechanism which failed to produce
   follow-through on three prior debriefs will work this time because it's written down.
6. Passing a cheap, low-bar kill test (one warm path found; one concrete answer on a first
   call) is a reliable signal that the underlying bet is worth real capacity, not just that
   the bar itself was easy to clear.
7. Funding Bet C in parallel "regardless of which capture bet wins" doesn't quietly compete
   with A/B/recompete for the same scarce senior attention and security-office throughput —
   a tension the synthesis's own cross-cutting tradeoffs section admits is unresolved.

**Load-bearing:**

- **#3** — if Bet A's target agency doesn't procure inside 18 months, the entire "run A and
  B in parallel" framing collapses to "run B, and separately decide whether to defend the
  recompete" — a different recommendation entirely.
- **#4** — if the recompete is actually near-term, Option 4 (defend the recompete directly)
  stops being the fallback caveat at the bottom of the note and becomes the headline; the
  recommendation's own "wrong if" clause admits this but the plan doesn't act on it yet.
- **#5** — if owner+date isn't actually the missing ingredient, this synthesis becomes the
  fourth diagnosed-and-ignored recommendation Theme 4 warns about, produced by the same
  session that named the pattern.

## Kill tests

- **#3:** Same-day desk check — pull the target agency's public budget justification /
  acquisition-forecast notice, or ask any existing cleared contact in that agency's
  contracting shop for the program's actual timeline. Cheaper and faster than either bet's
  own kill test; should have been run before either kill test was scheduled.
- **#4:** A 10-minute internal question to the contracts/capture team: when does the
  flagship recompete's RFP actually drop? This fact should already be known inside the
  company and costs nothing to surface — yet it's absent from the whole synthesis.
- **#5:** Check whether the two prior named debrief recommendations ("build the bench,"
  "pursue teaming") already had a named owner and date the first time they were written up.
  If they did and follow-through still didn't happen, "owner + date" is falsified as the fix
  before it's relied on again.

## Premortem — twelve months later, this failed

1. **Most probable:** both kill tests clear on a low bar (a warm intro surfaces, a call
   yields a vague "let's talk"), BD capacity gets split across A, B, and whatever the
   recompete needed — and six months in, neither new bet has produced a signed agreement or
   task order, while the recompete, which no one gave a dedicated resourced plan, is the
   thing that actually slips.
2. **Second:** the owner-and-date discipline looks good in the room on day one but by month
   three there is no standing review cadence forcing anyone back to it, so the same silent
   drop happens a fourth time — this synthesis becomes the next loss debrief's cited example
   of good diagnosis, no follow-through.
3. **Third:** Bet A's kill test clears (a warm path exists), but the target agency's budget
   cycle turns out not to land inside 18 months at all — real capacity was spent chasing a
   program that couldn't have procured anything relevant during the window that mattered,
   because nobody checked the timeline before running the test.

## Reframes

- Instead of "which bet(s) get resourced," ask: **what's the smallest resourcing split that
  doesn't touch the flagship recompete at all until its own timeline is confirmed** — treat
  the recompete as a zero-cost constraint to protect, not a soft consideration to weigh.
- Invert the follow-through fix: rather than "assign an owner and date" (the mechanism
  apparently already tried and already failed at least once), HMW build a recurring,
  calendared checkpoint owned by someone with *no stake* in either bet's outcome — an
  external forcing function instead of a self-policed one?
- Reframe "run both kill tests in parallel" as "run the cheapest disqualifying test first,
  sequentially" — order by which test yields the highest-value negative information per hour
  spent, rather than defaulting to parallel just because the calendars don't conflict.

## Steelman of the opposite

The clinic's stated goal was recompete pressure on the flagship contract, not two new
logos. Every hour of senior BD time spent probing a cold-start adjacent agency or courting a
competing prime is an hour not spent on the relationship that pays the bills today and whose
loss would dwarf either new bet's upside for years. Both new bets carry unresolved,
undated foundational uncertainties — an asserted-not-confirmed agency budget window, a
"no new headcount" resourcing ask from a BD bench the room never actually sized against the
recompete's own needs — while the recompete's own timeline was never even pulled during this
session, an omission stranger and more consequential than either new-channel gap the
synthesis lists under Gaps. A disciplined operator facing one unconfirmed near-term
existential risk and two unconfirmed speculative opportunities defends the existential risk
first and treats new-channel exploration as next quarter's project once recompete prep has
its own resourced, dated plan. Running two half-hearted kill tests in parallel while the
actual clock ticks unattended is the excitement-over-buildability failure mode the room
already diagnosed once today, just relocated one level up the decision stack.

## Verdict

**reshape** — do not run the parallel-kill-test plan until the flagship recompete's own
timeline is pulled and checked against the 18-month window; that single fact reorders the
entire options list, and it's cheaper to get than either bet's kill test.
