# Grow our own cleared bench by sponsoring clearances in-house

Hire junior, uncleared staff, sponsor their clearance, and bind them to a contractual commitment period — cheaper over time than hiring cleared staff laterally at market rate.

## Notes
- Crazy-8s origin: an explicit "cleared talent farm system" — hire junior uncleared staff, sponsor clearance, attach a contractual commitment period.
- Dot vote: 8 dots, third place, advanced to bet-brief writing as Bet C.
- Bet brief: framed as an internal capability, not customer-facing — "we grow our own cleared bench instead of renting it at market rate"; target is internal (recruiting pipeline, finance, the security office) rather than a customer; the writing group itself flagged this isn't really a capture tactic at all — it's an ops/talent bet, a different kind of bet than the two customer-facing ones. Resourcing ask was security-office clearance-sponsorship throughput, a signing-bonus budget, and an 18-24 month payback horizon.
- Bet-brief risk flagged by the group: the long payback period doesn't help this year's recompete pressure — it's more of a structural fix than a strategic bet, and the room debated whether it belonged in a full bet-brief treatment at all (see [[structural-ops-bets-need-different-criteria-than-bd-capture-bets]]).
- Decide gate: pulled out of the "strategic bet" frame entirely — agreed to be a real initiative, but evaluated on its own terms as an ops initiative rather than competing against the two customer-facing bets for the "strategic bet" label. Given its own action line (clearance-sponsorship pipeline capacity/cost scoping) rather than a go/no-go gate like the other two.
- Connects back to one of the day's warm-up stories: a past recompete loss was traced to never having built a bench in the first place, see [[loss-debriefs-recommendations-go-unactioned]].
