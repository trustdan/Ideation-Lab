# Challenge — reduce single-prime exposure through a second channel (Bet B)

The synthesis notes "no challenge targets this theme's mechanics directly" — only the
Theme-2 safer-bet steelman brushes against it in passing. Bet B advanced to bet-brief
writing with 9 dots and is one of the two live options in the recommendation; it has not
been pressure-tested to the same standard as Bet A.

## Assumption inventory

1. The optics risk (courting a prime while competing against them elsewhere) can be
   neutralized by careful framing — assumes the target prime's capture team experiences it
   as friendly diversification rather than exactly what it looks like.
2. The "reliable sub who never no-bids you" positioning is a real, verifiable, differentiated
   claim — assumes this company has, in fact, never no-bid its current prime, and that no
   other sub is already pitching the identical line to the same target primes.
3. Twelve months of relationship-building, with no forcing function beyond a first
   concrete-ask call, reliably converts to an actual teaming agreement inside the 18-month
   strategic window — i.e., that BD relationship time compounds into contractual value on
   this timeline without a deadline-linked capture opportunity driving it.
4. "No new headcount required" holds — assumes senior BD capacity has real slack, when the
   same leads are also implicated in Bet A's kill test and no one has evaluated what the
   flagship recompete itself needs from BD.
5. There is one clear, agreed "#2 player" to court — the bet-brief itself names *two*
   candidate primes, both currently competitors on live bids, without the room ever
   resolving which one is actually the target; the synthesis doesn't flag this as open.
6. Building a second prime relationship meaningfully reduces single-prime *dependence risk*
   in practice — assumes the new relationship carries genuinely different program or budget
   exposure, not just the same underlying customer or funding line one hop removed.

**Load-bearing:**

- **#1** — if the current prime notices and reacts before the courtship pays off (pulling
  back task orders, cooling teaming on a live bid), the bet damages the one revenue
  relationship funding everything else — a cost the safer-bet steelman gestures at in
  general but never quantifies against Theme 1 specifically.
- **#2** — if the "never no-bid" claim is untrue or unremarkable, twelve months of courting
  produces goodwill with no lever, exactly the top failure mode the idea's own premortem
  already names.
- **#5** — if the room never actually converged on one target, "Bet B" is quietly two
  half-speed pursuits sharing one senior BD lead's bandwidth, doubling the real resourcing
  ask the synthesis lists as "no new headcount."

## Kill tests

- **#1:** Before the first exploratory call, pull the current prime's live bid calendar —
  are there imminent submissions where this company's teaming enthusiasm visibly matters? If
  yes, a same-day internal conversation with whoever owns that relationship to gut-check the
  optics risk before testing it externally, rather than discovering it after the fact.
- **#2:** Same-day internal pull of this company's actual bid/no-bid history with the
  current prime over the last 2–3 years. Any recorded no-bid falsifies the pitch on day one
  and forces a different value proposition before the first call happens.
- **#5:** A 30-minute internal alignment check with VP Capture and the BD leads: is there one
  named target prime, or is the bet-brief quietly carrying two? Surface a resourcing-
  doubling problem now, not six months in.

## Premortem — twelve months later, this failed

1. **Most probable:** the "never no-bid" pitch turns out untrue or unremarkable, the
   relationship stays a friendly lunch with no forcing function, and a year later there's
   goodwill but no teaming agreement — while senior BD hours that could have gone to the
   flagship recompete were spent on relationship maintenance with no contractual output.
2. **Second:** the current prime notices the courtship — cleared-industry circles are small
   — and quietly cools teaming with this company on a live bid; the optics risk materializes
   as lost revenue on the relationship the company actually depends on today, not merely as
   reputational awkwardness.
3. **Third:** the room never resolved which of the two named candidate primes is "the"
   target, BD effort split across both, neither got enough attention to produce the first
   concrete ask, and the bet quietly became two underfunded pursuits instead of one resourced
   one.

## Reframes

- Instead of "court a #2 prime for 12 months," HMW get a paid, contracted signal of interest
  — a small task-order-ready teaming agreement or MOU — inside 90 days, so "goodwill with no
  teeth" can't be the failure mode? Collapse the timeline instead of extending it.
- Invert the positioning: instead of pitching "we never no-bid you" (a claim about the past
  that may not hold up or differentiate), HMW lead with a specific capability gap the target
  prime is already known to have, discovered before the first call rather than asserted in
  it?
- Reframe "pick the #2 player" as a portfolio move: HMW test both named candidates in
  parallel at low cost — one call each — precisely to resolve the "which one" ambiguity,
  rather than assuming the bet-brief's singular framing was ever settled?

## Steelman of the opposite

The honest case against Theme 1 as a strategic bet is that it optimizes for the wrong kind
of risk. Single-prime dependence is a real structural exposure, but a relationship with no
forcing function and no contractual mechanism is not diversification — it's a hope, dressed
in a bet-brief. The one thing definitely still true in twelve months is the current prime
relationship, warts and all, which is why the safer-bet steelman logged against Theme 2
applies here with at least equal force. It arguably applies with *more* force: Theme 2 at
least carries a scoped, binary kill test (a hard two-week network-probe result); Theme 1's
kill test — "ask for something concrete on the first call" — depends entirely on how a
conversation happens to go, and is far easier to read as a soft pass on weak evidence. If the
company can only run one truly rigorous experiment this quarter, the rigor gap between the
two bets' kill tests is itself an argument either for sharpening Bet B's test to Bet A's
standard before trusting it, or for ranking Bet B below both Bet A and the still-unscoped
case for defending the recompete directly.

## Verdict

**reshape** — Bet B doesn't proceed to its first exploratory call until the "never no-bid"
claim is checked against real bid history and the room confirms a single named target prime;
as written it risks converting BD goodwill into nothing, exactly as its own premortem
predicts.
