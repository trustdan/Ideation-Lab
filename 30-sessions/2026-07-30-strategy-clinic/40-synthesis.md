# Synthesis — 2026-07-30 strategy clinic

## Read me first

The pool says the room already did most of the hard work itself: eighteen ideas resolve
into three concrete, dot-voted bet-briefs (adjacent-mission cyber range, second-prime
courting, in-house cleared-talent bench) plus a standing warning — voiced three separate
times today in three different debriefs — that this company diagnoses its own weaknesses
correctly and then doesn't act on the fix. The central tension is that the most exciting bet
(adjacent-mission cyber range, the dot-vote landslide) is also the one with the least
existing traction (a genuine cold start), while the safer, cheaper bet (courting a second
prime) got real but lesser conviction and carries its own credibility risk. What has to be
decided today is not which idea is cleverest — it's which 1–2 of these three already-shaped
bets get an owner, a first action, and a date, and whether this time the follow-through
problem named in Theme 4 actually gets solved alongside them.

## Themes

### 1. Reduce single-prime exposure through a second channel

**Members:**

- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/court-a-second-prime-relationship-deliberately|Deliberately court a second prime for 12 months before we need them]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/sub-to-a-different-prime-to-spread-risk|Sub to a different prime in the same mission area to spread single-prime risk]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/position-as-a-surge-sub-for-thin-bench-primes|Market ourselves explicitly as a surge sub for primes with a thin bench]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/pursue-small-prime-awards-under-10m|Go direct on small prime awards under $10M, where the past-performance bar is lower]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/reframe-small-business-status-as-wedge|Small-business set-aside status is a wedge to press, not just a ceiling to escape]]

**Strongest variant:** Deliberately court a named #2 prime for 12 months, positioned as "the
reliable sub who never no-bids you" — 9 dots, advanced to bet-brief writing as Bet B, no new
headcount required, just senior BD time.

**Live tension:** the one variant the room actually backed (courting a specific #2 prime)
carries a credibility risk none of the weaker variants share — both candidate primes are
currently competitors on 1–2 live bids, so courting them as a future sub while competing
against them elsewhere can read as opportunistic or two-faced. The four other diversification
mechanisms in this theme (teaming reciprocity with whichever prime has room, surge-sub
branding, direct small awards, small-business status as leverage) all got real but
insufficient energy (0–5 dots) — they were sketched, not killed, and remain unexplored
fallback paths if Bet B's specific relationship goes cold.

**Open challenges:** no challenge targets this theme's mechanics directly, but
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/the-safer-bet-is-doubling-down-on-the-existing-prime|the safer-bet steelman]]
(logged under Theme 2, since it was raised against the cyber-range bet) argues on principle
against diluting BD capacity into *any* new relationship — new prime or new agency — while the
existing prime relationship still generates real revenue today. That argument bears on this
theme as much as on Theme 2's bet.

- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/sell-sustainment-data-analytics-not-labor|Sell the sustainment data layer, not the labor layer]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/rebrand-as-a-data-company|Reposition the whole company as a data company, not a labor company]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/pursue-training-and-simulation-adjacent-to-om|Extend into training and simulation work adjacent to current O&M contracts]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/dot-vote-winners-may-reflect-excitement-not-buildability|A dot-vote winner can reflect excitement rather than buildability]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/the-safer-bet-is-doubling-down-on-the-existing-prime|The safer bet is doubling down on the existing prime relationship, not chasing an unproven adjacent agency]]

**Strongest variant:** Pursue cyber range support in an adjacent agency, entering as an
accreditation subcontractor to the incumbent range integrator — 11 dots, the clear winner of
the entire shortlist, advanced to bet-brief writing as Bet A, targeting a program office that
starts budget planning next fiscal year, capture tactic is a teaming call within 60 days.

**Live tension:** the dot-vote landslide sits on top of zero existing relationships in the
target agency — a genuine cold start, flagged in the bet-brief itself. The three weaker
adjacent-offering variants (sustainment data-analytics, the blunter data-company rebrand,
training-and-sim adjacency) never found a mechanism as concrete as Bet A's; sustainment data
in particular was pushed hard verbally twice and still couldn't get the room's dots, and was
explicitly parked rather than killed for next quarter.

**Open challenges:** both challenges directly named in the session target this theme.
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/dot-vote-winners-may-reflect-excitement-not-buildability|The excitement-vs-buildability caution]]
was raised specifically about this bet's landslide vote.
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/the-safer-bet-is-doubling-down-on-the-existing-prime|The safer-bet steelman]]
visibly shook the room's enthusiasm mid-session by pointing out the flagship prime relationship
generates real revenue today while this bet risks burning a quarter of BD capacity on a
program that may never have been open to new entrants. The bet was kept alive, not dropped,
but gated on a kill test (a 2-week warm-path probe) precisely because of these two challenges.

**Strongest variant:** Sponsor clearances for junior, uncleared hires in-house with a
contractual commitment period — 8 dots, third place, advanced to bet-brief writing as Bet C,
security-office throughput plus signing-bonus budget, 18–24 month payback.

**Live tension:** this is the one shaped bet in the whole pool the room itself decided
*isn't* a strategic bet — it has no capture tactic, no customer, and a payback horizon that
does nothing for this year's recompete pressure. It also sits oddly next to the room's own
retention data: exit interviews independently and repeatedly named flexibility, not
compensation, as the top retention lever, yet two of this theme's other supporting ideas
(equity-like win bonuses, a fractional bench) are still comp/staffing mechanisms rather than a
flexibility policy — the theme's most-voted fix and its own retention data point in different
directions.

**Open challenges:**

with its own action line (clearance-pipeline capacity/cost scoping) rather than judging it
head-to-head against the two customer-facing bets.

### 4. Close the loop on diagnosed weaknesses

**Members:**

- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/loss-debriefs-recommendations-go-unactioned|Loss debriefs keep naming the real fix, and the org keeps not doing it]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/proposals-fail-when-written-to-outdated-conops|Proposals lose when they're written to the customer's old CONOPS instead of their current one]]

**Strongest variant:** three separate contract losses — thin bench never built after a
sustainment recompete debrief said so, no teaming approach ever pursued after an IDIQ loss
that explicitly recommended one, and a proposal written to a stale CONOPS — each produced a
clear, specific, correct recommendation that was never acted on before the same weakness
resurfaced.

**Live tension:** this theme has no bet-brief of its own and wasn't dot-voted; it was used
as the day's framing device rather than competed against the other three bets. That means its
core risk — good diagnosis without follow-through — is not itself gated by anything in
today's process. Whatever gets committed from Themes 1–3 inherits this exact risk unless
something changes about how recommendations get tracked this time.

**Open challenges:** none raised directly against this theme, which is itself the implicit
challenge standing behind every other theme.

## Cross-cutting tradeoffs

1. **Excitement vs. buildability, at every vote.** The clear dot-vote winner (Theme 2's
   cyber-range bet, 11 dots) has zero existing relationships in its target agency; the
   second-place bet the room actually shaped into a plan (Theme 1's second-prime courting,
   9 dots) carries a credibility risk not stress-tested with as sharp a kill test. Vote count
   alone does not track readiness in either case.
2. **New-channel bets vs. defending the flagship prime.** The steelman raised against Theme
   2 — that the existing prime relationship generates real revenue today and a new bet risks
   diverting scarce BD capacity — applies with nearly equal force to Theme 1. No idea in the
   pool proposes directly strengthening the flagship recompete itself as an alternative to
   either new-channel bet; that omission is a gap (see below), not a resolved tradeoff.
3. **Strategic bets vs. structural fixes racing the same clock.** Theme 3's cleared-talent
   bench is a sound fix with an 18–24 month payback that the room correctly refused to judge
   against Themes 1–2's customer-facing capture tactics — but it still competes with them for
   the same finite senior BD and security-office attention inside the same 18-month window
   Theme 4 says the org has a track record of losing.

## Gaps

- **No bet-brief for defending the flagship recompete directly.** Given the recompete
  pressure named in the session goal, and the steelman raised in Theme 2, it's notable that
  no one proposed a concrete move to shore up the existing prime relationship or the recompete
  bid itself — every shaped idea points outward at a new channel, agency, or offering.
- **No fix for the specific bid-craft failure Theme 4 names.** "Proposals fail when written to
  an outdated CONOPS" is a named, diagnosed root cause with no corresponding proposal-review
  or customer-currency process idea anywhere in the pool to prevent a fourth recurrence.
- **No teaming/past-performance mechanism for the specific historical loss cited.** The
  IDIQ loss debrief literally recommended "consider a teaming approach to establish past
  performance" — Theme 1's ideas are adjacent to this but none is written as a direct answer
  to that named gap (a formal teaming agreement or performance-based subcontract structure).
- **No accountability mechanism for Theme 4 itself.** The pool diagnoses the
  diagnose-and-don't-act pattern three times over but contains no idea about *how* to make
  follow-through different this time (an owner/date tracking habit, a standing review
  cadence, etc.) — the session's own success criteria (owner + date) is the closest thing to
  an answer, and it lives in `50-actions.md`, not in this idea pool.
- **No pricing, margin, or cost-structure idea.** Recompete pressure is typically a cost
  competitiveness problem as much as a relationship or mission-fit problem; nothing in the
  pool touches indirect rates, staffing cost structure, or price-to-win.

## Recommendation & decision framing

**What to decide:** which 1–2 of the three already-shaped bet-briefs — Theme 2's cyber-range
adjacent-mission bet (A), Theme 1's second-prime courting bet (B) — become the named
strategic bet(s) for the next 18 months, with Theme 3's cleared-talent bench (C) funded in
parallel as an ops initiative regardless of that choice (the room already reclassified it out
of the strategic-bet competition), and with Theme 4's follow-through problem addressed
explicitly rather than left as background risk.

**The real options:**

1. **Commit to Bet A alone** (adjacent-mission cyber range) — highest upside, highest
   excitement, but a genuine cold start; already gated on a 2-week warm-path kill test.
2. **Commit to Bet B alone** (second-prime courting) — lower resourcing ask (senior BD time
   only), lower excitement, real relationship-optics risk; already gated on a first-call
   concrete-ask kill test.
3. **Run both A and B in parallel through their kill tests** before committing further BD
   capacity to either — both kill tests are cheap and don't compete for the same calendar slot
   (a network probe vs. a single exploratory call), so this doesn't require choosing blind
   today.
4. **Reject both new-channel bets** and instead defend the flagship recompete directly, per
   the safer-bet steelman — the option the pool names as an argument but never turns into its
   own bet-brief or capture tactic (see Gaps).

**Criteria to decide by:**

- Which bet clears its kill test first — both tests are already designed and cheap; use them
  as the forcing function instead of choosing on vote count.
- Whether senior BD capacity can actually run both kill tests without starving either or the
  flagship recompete itself.
- Fit to the 18-month window: Bet A's target program office starts budget planning "next
  fiscal year" — confirm this lands inside the window before treating it as this cycle's bet.
- Whether the follow-through problem named in Theme 4 gets a concrete answer this time — an
  owner and a date in `50-actions.md`, not just a decide-gate note.

**Recommendation:** Run both kill tests in parallel over the next 2–4 weeks — they are cheap,
don't compete for the same time slot, and were designed for exactly this purpose — before
committing real BD capacity to either bet; fund Theme 3's cleared-talent bench as a parallel
ops initiative regardless of which capture bet wins; and assign an explicit owner and 30-day
check-in date to whichever bet(s) survive their kill tests, specifically so this doesn't
become the fourth diagnosed-and-ignored recommendation Theme 4 warns about. This
recommendation is wrong if BD leads report they cannot resource two parallel kill tests
without dropping other live pursuits (in which case run Bet B's single low-cost call first),
or if the flagship recompete falls inside a 6-month window rather than the full 18 (in which
case Option 4 — defending the recompete directly — deserves its own bet-brief before either
new-channel bet, since neither A nor B is built to pay off before Bet A's own target agency
even starts budget planning).
