# Synthesis — 2026-07-30 strategy clinic

## Read me first

Eighteen ideas collapse into three shaped, dot-voted bet-briefs — an adjacent-mission cyber
range play, a deliberate second-prime courtship, and an in-house cleared-talent bench — sitting
on top of a fourth, unvoted theme naming a pattern the room admits about itself: it correctly
diagnoses its own weaknesses in loss debriefs and then doesn't act on the fix. The central
tension is that every open question raised against the two competing customer-facing bets — a
cold-start adjacent agency with an unconfirmed budget window and unconfirmed demand, versus a
second-prime courtship with an unresolved optics risk and an unverified pitch — is answerable
with cheap, same-week desk checks that nobody ran before the room reached for the more
expensive kill tests already designed. What has to be decided today is not which bet is more
exciting; it's whether leadership resources the cheap disqualifying checks first, which 1–2 of
the three shaped bets survive them, and whether the follow-through problem Theme 4 names gets a
mechanism this time instead of another good diagnosis.

## Themes

### 1. Reduce single-prime exposure through a second channel

**Members:**

- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/court-a-second-prime-relationship-deliberately|Deliberately court a second prime for 12 months before we need them]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/sub-to-a-different-prime-to-spread-risk|Sub to a different prime in the same mission area to spread single-prime risk]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/position-as-a-surge-sub-for-thin-bench-primes|Market ourselves explicitly as a surge sub for primes with a thin bench]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/pursue-small-prime-awards-under-10m|Go direct on small prime awards under $10M, where the past-performance bar is lower]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/reframe-small-business-status-as-wedge|Small-business set-aside status is a wedge to press, not just a ceiling to escape]]

**Strongest variant:** Deliberately court a named #2 prime for 12 months, pitched as "the
reliable sub who never no-bids you" — 9 dots, written up as Bet B: no new headcount, senior BD
time only, gated on a first-call kill test (ask for one concrete task order, not "let's stay in
touch").

**Live tension:** the bet-brief itself never resolved which of two named candidate primes is
actually "the" target — both are current competitors on live bids, so courting either carries
an optics risk the writing group flagged but didn't quantify. The four weaker diversification
variants in this theme (teaming reciprocity with whichever prime has room, surge-sub branding,
direct small awards, small-business status as leverage) drew real but insufficient energy
(0–5 dots) and remain sketched, unexplored fallbacks rather than killed ideas.

**Open challenges:**
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/challenge-reduce-single-prime-exposure-through-a-second-channel|A dedicated challenge]]
found this bet under-tested relative to Bet A: the "never no-bid" claim is asserted, not
verified against this company's actual bid history; the two-candidate ambiguity means "no new
headcount" may quietly mean two half-funded pursuits; and Bet B's own kill test ("ask for
something concrete") is a softer, more subjective bar than Bet A's binary network-probe test —
an asymmetry the challenge's steelman argues should rank Bet B *below* Bet A, not alongside it,
until sharpened. Verdict: **reshape** — confirm bid history and a single named target before the
first exploratory call.

**Strongest variant:** Pursue cyber range support in an adjacent agency, entering as an
accreditation subcontractor to the incumbent range integrator — 11 dots, the runaway winner of

fiscal year," capture tactic is a teaming call within 60 days, gated on a 2-week warm-path
network probe.

**Live tension:** the landslide vote sits on a genuine cold start — zero existing relationships
in the target agency — and on an unverified premise that the incumbent integrator actually has
an accreditation gap to fill at all. The three weaker adjacent-offering variants (sustainment
data-analytics, the blunter data-company rebrand, training-and-sim adjacency) never found a
mechanism as concrete as Bet A's; sustainment data was pushed hard verbally twice and still
couldn't get the room's dots, and was explicitly parked rather than killed for next quarter.

**Open challenges:** three challenges bear on this theme.

[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/the-safer-bet-is-doubling-down-on-the-existing-prime|The safer-bet steelman]]
argued the existing prime relationship pays the bills today and shouldn't be diluted for a
speculative cold start; it visibly shook the room's enthusiasm mid-session.
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/challenge-expand-into-an-adjacent-mission-or-offering|A third, more targeted challenge]]

the room's 11-to-9 vote a bet on the harder-to-verify option. Verdict: **reshape** — run a
same-week demand check (does the integrator actually lack accreditation capability) before
spending any of the network-probe cycle.

- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/structural-ops-bets-need-different-criteria-than-bd-capture-bets|Structural/ops bets need different evaluation criteria than BD capture bets]]

**Strongest variant:** Sponsor clearances for junior, uncleared hires in-house with a
contractual commitment period — 8 dots, third place, written up as Bet C: security-office
throughput plus signing-bonus budget, 18–24 month payback.

**Live tension:** this is the one shaped bet the room itself decided isn't really a strategic
bet — no capture tactic, no customer, a payback horizon that does nothing for this year's
recompete pressure. It also sits oddly next to its own supporting data: exit interviews
independently and repeatedly named flexibility, not compensation, as the top retention lever,
yet two of the theme's other ideas (win-tied bonuses, a fractional bench) are still comp/staffing
mechanisms rather than a flexibility policy — the theme's most-voted fix and its own evidence
point in different directions. The freshly-verdicted test scenario against this theme (see
Scenario verdicts, below) sharpens that point: the specific fractional-bench mechanism is
directly contradicted by the theme's own exit-interview evidence.

**Open challenges:**
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/structural-ops-bets-need-different-criteria-than-bd-capture-bets|The structural-vs-capture challenge]]
targets this bet directly; the room accepted it and reclassified the bet as an ops initiative
with its own action line rather than judging it head-to-head against the two customer-facing
bets. No further challenge disputes that reclassification.

### 4. Close the loop on diagnosed weaknesses

**Members:**

- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/loss-debriefs-recommendations-go-unactioned|Loss debriefs keep naming the real fix, and the org keeps not doing it]]
- [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/proposals-fail-when-written-to-outdated-conops|Proposals lose when they're written to the customer's old CONOPS instead of their current one]]

**Strongest variant:** three separate contract losses — a thin bench never built after a
sustainment recompete debrief said so, no teaming approach ever pursued after an IDIQ loss that
explicitly recommended one, and a proposal written to a stale CONOPS — each produced a clear,
specific, correct recommendation that was never acted on before the same weakness resurfaced.

**Live tension:** this theme has no bet-brief of its own and was never dot-voted; it was used as
the day's framing device rather than competed against the other three bets. That means its core
risk — good diagnosis without follow-through — isn't gated by anything in today's process.
Whatever gets committed from Themes 1–3 inherits this exact risk unless something changes about
how recommendations get tracked this time.

**Open challenges:** none targets this theme directly, but
[[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/challenge-recommendation|the challenge against the recommendation]]
turns it back on the whole session: it points out that "assign an owner and a date" is the
mechanism apparently already tried on at least one of the two named prior losses, and proposes
checking whether it was present and still failed — if so, owner+date is falsified as the fix
before this session relies on it again.

## Cross-cutting tradeoffs

1. **Cheap verification vs. expensive kill tests, on both live bets.** Every open question
   raised against Bet A (does the integrator have a real gap, does the agency's budget window
   land inside 18 months) and Bet B (is the "never no-bid" claim true, is there one target prime
   or two) is answerable with a same-day or same-week desk check or internal call — cheaper and
   faster than either bet's own designed kill test (a 2-week network probe; a first exploratory
   call). The room designed rigorous-looking tests before pulling the cheap facts that could
   make those tests unnecessary. The same pattern now shows up formally: the freshly-verdicted
   Bet-B test scenario (below) lands **inconclusive** for exactly this reason — the doubt is
   about unverified assumptions, not disproven ones.
2. **New-channel bets vs. defending the flagship prime.** The safer-bet steelman raised against
   Theme 2 — the existing prime relationship generates real revenue today and a new bet risks
   diverting scarce BD capacity — applies with comparable, arguably greater, force to Theme 1,
   whose own kill test is the softer of the two. No idea in the pool proposes directly
   strengthening the flagship recompete itself as an alternative to either new-channel bet, and
   the recompete's own timeline was never pulled during the session at all — a gap sharper than
   either bet's individually-flagged uncertainty (see Gaps).
3. **Strategic bets vs. structural fixes racing the same clock.** Theme 3's cleared-talent bench
   is a sound fix with an 18–24 month payback that the room correctly refused to judge against
   Themes 1–2's customer-facing capture tactics — but it still competes with them for the same
   finite senior BD and security-office attention inside the same 18-month window Theme 4 says
   the org has a track record of losing. The one piece of this theme now actually tested against
   the pool's own evidence — the fractional-bench mechanism — comes back **refuted**, which
   narrows, not widens, what Theme 3 should spend that scarce attention on.

## Gaps

- **No bet-brief for defending the flagship recompete directly, and its timeline was never
  pulled.** Given the recompete pressure named in the session goal, nobody surfaced when the
  flagship RFP actually drops — a fact that should cost nothing to check internally and that
  reorders every option below if the answer is "soon."
- **No fix for the specific bid-craft failure Theme 4 names.** "Proposals fail when written to
  an outdated CONOPS" is a named, diagnosed root cause with no corresponding proposal-review or
  customer-currency process idea anywhere in the pool to prevent a fourth recurrence.
- **No teaming/past-performance mechanism for the specific historical loss cited.** The IDIQ
  loss debrief literally recommended "consider a teaming approach to establish past
  performance" — Theme 1's ideas are adjacent to this but none is written as a direct answer to
  that named gap (a formal teaming agreement or performance-based subcontract structure).
- **No accountability mechanism for Theme 4 itself, and the one proposed fix is unverified.**
  The pool diagnoses the diagnose-and-don't-act pattern three times over, and this session's own
  default fix — an owner and a date — may already have been tried on a prior debrief and still
  failed; nothing in the pool proposes an *external*, no-stake-holding check on follow-through.
- **No pricing, margin, or cost-structure idea.** Recompete pressure is typically a cost
  competitiveness problem as much as a relationship or mission-fit problem; nothing in the pool
  touches indirect rates, staffing cost structure, or price-to-win.
- **No replacement idea for the now-refuted fractional-bench mechanism.** Ts-02 (below) finds
  the pool's own evidence points at flexible/remote work, not a fractional consultant bench, as
  the actual retention lever — but nothing in Theme 3 turns that exit-interview finding into a
  concrete flexibility policy or process; the correct lever is named, not built.

## Scenario verdicts

Two test scenarios were hand-added to this session since the last synthesis pass, each carrying
its own `sprint-question:`. Both are now assigned a verdict against the idea pool (frontmatter
updated on the notes themselves; summarized here per §5.2/§7).

| ID | Sprint question | Verdict | Evidence cited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ts-01 | Will pursuing direct small-prime awards and a second prime relationship meaningfully reduce our single-prime revenue concentration within 18 months? | **inconclusive** | [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/challenge-reduce-single-prime-exposure-through-a-second-channel\|Challenge — reduce single-prime exposure (Bet B)]]; [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/pursue-small-prime-awards-under-10m\|Go direct on small prime awards under $10M]] |
| ts-02 | Will building a fractional cleared-consultant bench improve cleared-talent retention enough to protect win rate on recompetes? | **refuted** | [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/flexible-remote-work-is-the-top-cleared-retention-lever\|Flexible or remote work is the top cleared-retention lever]]; [[30-sessions/2026-07-30-strategy-clinic/30-ideas/build-a-fractional-cleared-consultant-bench\|Build a fractional cleared-consultant bench]] |

**ts-01 reasoning.** The bundled claim rests on two very different pieces of evidence. The
second-prime courtship (Bet B, 9 dots) is real but unverified: its own dedicated challenge shows
the "never no-bid" pitch is asserted rather than checked against bid history, the bet-brief
never resolved which of two named candidate primes is the actual target, and the kill test is a
soft, subjective bar. Its premortem's most-probable failure mode is a year of goodwill with no
signed teaming agreement — a real warning, but the challenge's own verdict is **reshape**, not
**park**: it's recoverable pending cheap verification, not disproven. The small-prime-awards
half of the claim, meanwhile, was never developed at all — it scored in the bottom dot-vote tier
(0–1 dots) and has no bet-brief, kill test, or premortem behind it. There isn't enough pool
evidence to call the combined claim validated, and there isn't a decisive disqualification
either — the honest call is **inconclusive**, pending the bid-history and single-target checks
the challenge already specifies.

**ts-02 reasoning.** This scenario names one specific mechanism — a *fractional* cleared-
consultant bench — not the committed Bet C (in-house clearance sponsorship for junior hires),
which is a different mechanism entirely and was not what the sprint question asked about. The
fractional-bench idea itself drew almost no room conviction (bottom dot-vote tier, 0–1 dots,
never bet-briefed). More directly, the pool contains a specific, repeated, independently-
corroborated finding from actual exit interviews: flexibility/remote work, not a comp or
staffing-structure lever, is what departing cleared staff cite as the top reason. A fractional
bench is a staffing/comp mechanism, not a flexibility policy — it is the type of fix the pool's
own data says isn't the one that moves retention. Between the low room conviction and the direct
contradicting evidence, this is a clean **refuted**, not merely inconclusive.

## Recommendation & decision framing

**What to decide:** which 1–2 of the three shaped bet-briefs — Theme 2's adjacent-mission cyber
range bet (A) and Theme 1's second-prime courtship bet (B) — become the named strategic bet(s)
for the next 18 months, with Theme 3's cleared-talent bench (C) funded in parallel as an ops
initiative regardless of that choice, and with Theme 4's follow-through problem answered by
something more than an owner and a date.

**The real options:**

1. **Run the cheap disqualifying checks first, on all three open fronts, before either kill
   test.** Pull the flagship recompete's actual RFP timeline (internal, same-day); check whether
   the incumbent range integrator has a real, unmet accreditation gap (one technical-contact
   call, same week); verify this company's actual bid/no-bid history with its current prime and
   confirm a single named target for Bet B (internal, same week) — the same checks that would
   also resolve ts-01's inconclusive verdict. None of this touches BD capacity earmarked for the
   bets themselves.
2. **Commit to Bet A alone** after it clears its (possibly sharpened) kill test — highest
   upside, highest excitement, but the harder-to-verify cold start.
3. **Commit to Bet B alone** after it clears a sharpened kill test — lower resourcing ask, lower
   excitement, an optics risk and an unresolved single-target question.
4. **Reject both new-channel bets** and defend the flagship recompete directly — the option the
   pool argues for but never turns into its own bet-brief, and the one option whose urgency
   can't be judged until the recompete's timeline is actually pulled.

**Criteria to decide by:**

- Whether the recompete's RFP timeline lands inside 6 months (making Option 4 the headline, not
  the footnote) or comfortably inside the 18-month window (leaving A/B live).
- Whether the cheap demand-check on Bet A and the bid-history/single-target check on Bet B
  disqualify either bet outright before a single hour of the more expensive kill tests is spent
  — the same check that would move ts-01 off **inconclusive**.
- Whether senior BD capacity, once sized against all three claims on it (Bet A, Bet B, and the
  recompete itself), can actually run more than one pursuit without starving the others.
- Whether the follow-through fix goes further than "owner and date" — specifically, whether
  those two prior debrief recommendations already had an owner and a date and still didn't get
  done.
- Whether Theme 3's ops initiative gets rebuilt around the retention lever the pool's evidence
  actually supports (flexible/remote work) now that the fractional-bench variant is refuted,
  rather than continuing to fund a comp/staffing mechanism the exit interviews already argue
  against.

**Recommendation:** Before spending any BD capacity on either bet's own kill test, run the four
cheap checks in Option 1 this week — they are same-day-to-same-week, internal or single-call,
and collectively cheaper than either bet's designed test. Let the recompete-timeline check set
the frame: if the RFP is near-term, treat defending the recompete as this cycle's actual bet and
push A/B to next quarter's shortlist; if it's comfortably inside 18 months, proceed to sharpened
kill tests for whichever of A/B survives its cheap check, run one at a time if BD capacity can't
absorb both. Fund Theme 3's cleared-talent bench as a parallel ops initiative regardless of the
capture-bet outcome, but redirect its retention piece toward flexible/remote-work policy rather
than the now-refuted fractional-consultant mechanism — the pool's own evidence names the actual
lever. Replace "owner and a 30-day check-in" with a recurring, calendared follow-through review
owned by someone with no stake in either bet's outcome — the cheapest, most direct answer to
Theme 4's warning that this session not become the fourth diagnosed-and-ignored recommendation.
This recommendation is wrong if the four cheap checks turn out to be harder or slower to get
than assumed here (in which case the original parallel-kill-test plan is the fallback, run on
the shorter runway remaining), or if the recompete timeline turns out to be irrelevant to this
quarter's resourcing question entirely (e.g., it's already fully staffed and funded independent
of BD), in which case Option 1's first check can be dropped and A/B evaluated on their own
merits alone.
